# Glossary — The Minimalist Entrepreneur (Sahil Lavingia)

> 关键概念词典。所有下游 extractor / skill 必须以本词典为准。
> 提取原则:作者反复使用 ≥3 次 / 作者显式定义 / 看起来像常识但作者用法不同 / 是核心论点的构件。

---

- id: g01
  term: Minimalist Entrepreneur
  type: term
  source_chapter: 第1章 The Minimalist Entrepreneur
  author_definition: |
    "I call these people 'minimalist entrepreneurs,' and I call their companies 'minimalist businesses.'"
    作者用以命名一类特定的创业者:以盈利为第一目标、不追求指数增长、
    保持对企业和个人生活控制权、并以服务一个特定社区为出发点的人。
    "Building a minimalist business does not mean settling for second best. Instead,
    it's about creating sustainable companies that have the flexibility to take risks
    to serve the greater good."
  key_distinction: |
    ≠ 字典义的"极简主义者"(减少日常物品、追求 less is more 的生活美学)
    ≠ "想做大但还没做大"的初创公司
    = 一整套 8 步方法论的执行者——盈利优先、社区先行、最小化构建、
      亲售前 100 客户、做你自己营销、有节制成长、自定义文化。
    作者的"minimalist"不是说"少做事",而是"用最少必要资源做该做的事",
    关键在 "right-size"(刚好合适)。
  why_it_matters: |
    全书的命名核心。下游 skill 如果不锚定这个术语,会把 Sahil 当成
    "Tim Ferriss 4-Hour Workweek 式的躺平生活家"或者"lifestyle business 鼓吹者",
    完全错失"盈利 + 增长 + 可持续 + 文化"四元一体的方法论。
  tags: [core-concept, person, philosophy]

- id: g02
  term: Minimalist Business
  type: term
  source_chapter: 第1章 The Minimalist Entrepreneur
  author_definition: |
    "Minimalist businesses... aim to be profitable from day one or soon after,
    because profit is oxygen for businesses. And they do that by selling a product
    to customers, not by selling their users to advertisers."
    "There are no multi-million-dollar exit strategies, no IPOs, no elite VCs
    on the cap table. Just a great product that people want, that makes money,
    that the founders are proud of."
  key_distinction: |
    ≠ 字典义的"小企业"(small business 通常意味"还没长大")
    ≠ "lifestyle business"(后者常被作者视为放弃野心的代名词)
    = 主动选择的 "right-size"——既不过小(无法覆盖创始团队生活),
      也不过度膨胀(为增长牺牲利润)。
      关键判定:"Gumroad may have been a crappy investment for a few venture
      capitalists, but it was still a great company for its customers."
  why_it_matters: |
    这个术语把"盈利+可持续+创始人控制权"打包成一个名词,下游 skill
    命名(如 "minimalist-business-bootstrapping-framework") 必须保留这个
    三元一体结构,不能拆开。
  tags: [core-concept, business-model]

- id: g03
  term: Profitability First / Profit is Oxygen
  type: term
  source_chapter: 第1章 Chase Profitability, Not Unicorns
  author_definition: |
    "Minimalist entrepreneurs create businesses that are profitable at all costs...
    profit is oxygen for businesses. And they do that by selling a product to
    customers, not by selling their users to advertisers."
    "We're laser focused on profitability from day one, in order to get to
    sustainability soon after, so that we can serve our customers and our
    communities for as long as we wish."
    同一意象在第6章再次出现:"Profitability gets you off the grid, allowing
    you to grow mindfully with unlimited runway."
  key_distinction: |
    ≠ "先烧钱增长,再考虑盈利"的硅谷 VC 范式
    ≠ 把盈利当作"终极目标"(作者说它是 "oxygen"——生存条件,不是目标)
    = 第一指标、第一天就要。从定义上排除"靠 VC 续命"和"靠广告卖用户"。
  why_it_matters: |
    这是全书最强的隐喻,出现 ≥10 次。下游任何 "MVP"/"财务"skill 都必须
    把"盈利 first"作为前置约束,而不是与"增长"并列的可选项。如果误读为
    "最终追求盈利",整个 8 步法的优先级就反了。
  tags: [core-concept, financial, metaphor]

- id: g04
  term: Chase Profitability, Not Unicorns
  type: term
  source_chapter: 第1章 Chase Profitability, Not Unicorns
  author_definition: |
    "Building a minimalist business is not a get-rich-quick proposition, but it
    is a get-rich-slowly one if you embrace profitability, not growth, as the
    key indicator of your company's success."
    "Many winners are just the last ones standing. Don't give up."
  key_distinction: |
    ≠ "不增长"(作者明确反对停止增长)
    ≠ "反对 VC"(作者后来自己也重新融过资,且做 VC LP)
    = 把"利润"而非"估值/规模"作为核心指标,接受"慢慢变富"。
      隐含的反命题:"Get big or die" 的硅谷主流叙事。
  why_it_matters: |
    这是作者定义"极简"的核心反义——不是反对做大,而是反对
    "以指数增长为默认假设"的创业文化。下游"VC vs Bootstrap 决策"skill
    必须用这个术语作为对照轴。
  tags: [core-concept, thesis-statement]

- id: g05
  term: Start with Community
  type: term
  source_chapter: 第2章 Start with Community
  author_definition: |
    "Minimalist entrepreneurs build on a foundation of community. They don't
    ask 'How can I help?' but are instead observant and cultivate authentic
    relationships."
    "It's the community that leads you to the problem, which leads you to the
    product, which leads you to your business."
    顺序是反的:不是 "我有 idea → 找市场",而是"我属于这个社区 →
    看到痛点 → 做产品"。
  key_distinction: |
    ≠ 字典义的"社区营销"(把已有的产品推给某个社群)
    ≠ 字典义的"目标客户"(target customer 是抽象标签,community 是真实的人)
    = 你已经深度参与的、彼此信任的、有共同兴趣/价值观/能力的人群。
      强调"先成为社区一员,再从这个位置上看机会"。
  why_it_matters: |
    是 8 步法的第 1 步。下游 "community-first-product-discovery" skill 必须
    把"先加入社区并做出贡献 ≥1 年"作为前置条件,否则就退化成普通的
    "市场细分"流程。
  tags: [core-concept, sequence, philosophy]

- id: g06
  term: Community vs. Network
  type: term
  source_chapter: 第2章 Start with Community
  author_definition: |
    "It's important to note that your goal here is to join communities, not
    networks. In a network, such as Facebook, Twitter, or Instagram, new-
    comers start at zero. No one says 'hi' when they walk in the door...
    Networks... aren't bad. Sometimes they can lead to genuine and meaningful
    connection... But where did those friends and followers come from in the
    first place? The communities you're in!"
  key_distinction: |
    ≠ 许多作者互换使用 "community" 和 "network"
    = Community:你长期参与、深度互惠、彼此认识的人际群。
      Network:你能单向触达的大众(followers / subscribers / 邮箱列表)。
      顺序是 community → network,**不能反过来**。
  why_it_matters: |
    下游 "audience-building" skill 不能直接教 "涨粉",必须先经过社区阶段。
    否则就复制了 Quibi 式失败:大流量,零转化。
  tags: [core-concept, distinction]

- id: g07
  term: Job to be Done (JTBD, reframed)
  type: term
  source_chapter: 第2章 Start with Community
  author_definition: |
    借用 Christensen 的概念,但作者明确批评原版的用法顺序:
    "Christensen's idea of 'the job to be done' is sound, but McDonald's is
    doing it in the wrong order. They didn't start with the customer; they
    started with the job to be done, and then sank a ton of money into making
    customers believe they needed that job to be done.
    Minimalist entrepreneurs... believe that people already have enough
    problems, and that our role is to help them get rid of one."
    作者改造:JTBD 应该是"发现(discover)社区里已经存在的痛点",而非
    "创造(make up)一个 job 然后营销给大众"。
  key_distinction: |
    ≠ Christensen 经典用法(允许通过营销"创造"job)
    = 必须先在已有社区里观察到的真实 job,不能反向制造需求。
  why_it_matters: |
    下游 "problem-validation" skill 必须把"已经在社区里被人提起的痛点"
    作为真信号,把"创始人自以为的痛点"作为伪信号。这个区分能避免
    "造需求"的失败模式。
  tags: [concept, borrowed, reframe]

- id: g08
  term: Processize (作者自造词)
  type: term
  source_chapter: 第3章 Build as Little as Possible
  author_definition: |
    "Unfortunately, the English language does not have a word for this activity,
    so I made one up: processize (verb) — to turn into a process: After they
    tested it on their friends, they processized their recommendation system."
    紧接其后作者立刻区分:"We don't have to make up a new word for this
    because it already exists: 'productizing.'"
  key_distinction: |
    ≠ 字典没有(作者自创)
    = 把一个帮单个客户解决问题的过程,变成可重复的步骤清单(process)。
      下一步才是 productize(把过程自动化成产品)。
      **processize 先于 productize**——这个顺序是 hard rule。
  why_it_matters: |
    这个词是作者方法论的核心动词。下游 "MVP 手动验证" skill 必须把
    "先 processize,再 productize"作为显式阶段,不能跳过手动阶段直接
    写代码(就是作者批评的"从 problem 直接跳到 product"的失败模式)。
  tags: [core-concept, coined-verb, sequence]

- id: g09
  term: Manual Valuable Process (MVP 的另一层意思)
  type: term
  source_chapter: 第3章 Build as Little as Possible
  author_definition: |
    "This document will be the true MVP of your business. I'm not talking about
    the minimum viable product that we're all trying to build and to launch.
    I'm talking about the manual valuable process that precedes it and will be
    the foundation for the business you're trying to build."
    Endcrawl 案例:创始人先用 Google Sheet + Perl 脚本,人工跑 6 步,
    "for filmmakers used to waiting up to twenty-four hours, it was a
    revelation that this process, even manually, took only about fifteen minutes."
  key_distinction: |
    ≠ 硅谷常见的 MVP(minimum viable product = 最小可发布产品)
    = 最小可售卖过程——可能根本没有代码,纯人工流程,只要能解决
      客户问题并收到钱即可。这是 MVP 的更前置阶段。
  why_it_matters: |
    全书最容易被误读的术语之一。下游 skill 必须区分:
    (1) Manual Valuable Process(过程级 MVP,无代码)→
    (2) Minimum Viable Product(产品级 MVP,有代码)→
    (3) Product-Market Fit(repeat customers)。
    三者顺序不可乱。
  tags: [core-concept, redefined-acronym, sequence]

- id: g10
  term: Productize (与 Processize 配对)
  type: term
  source_chapter: 第3章 Build as Little as Possible
  author_definition: |
    "Productizing simply means developing a process into something you can
    sell... you automate each individual task so that people can sign up, use,
    and pay for your product without you being involved.
    If processizing is how you scale a manual process, then productizing is how
    you go fully automatic."
  key_distinction: |
    ≠ 字典义的"商业化"(commercialize,可指任何变现)
    = 把手工流程的每一步自动化,变成客户自助使用的产品。
      是 processize 的下游步骤,不是同义词。
  why_it_matters: |
    下游任何"如何把咨询/服务做成 SaaS"的 skill 必须分两步:
    先 processize(写流程文档),再 productize(自动化)。混用这两个词
    会让"手工→自动化"的中间步骤丢失。
  tags: [core-concept, paired-with-processize]

- id: g11
  term: Right-Size Business
  type: term
  source_chapter: 第1章 + 第8章 Where Do We Go from Here?
  author_definition: |
    "This book, part manifesto, part manual, will help you design, build, and
    successfully grow your own right-size business."
    "I'm not trying to build a billion-dollar business at all costs... I could
    focus on making Gumroad better for our existing creators."
    "Some say that you need to grow like crazy, because 'if you don't get big,
    someone else will eat you.' As if companies were fish. This is wrong.
    The vast majority of small businesses are never eaten. Big fish want to
    eat other big fish."
  key_distinction: |
    ≠ "right-sizing"(HR 用语,指裁员)
    ≠ "停止增长"
    = 由创始人自主定义的"刚好够大"——足以覆盖团队生活和服务社区,
      但不为了规模牺牲利润或控制权。Big fish 不会吃小鱼。
  why_it_matters: |
    "Right-size" 是作者对"minimalist"的正向翻译。下游 "growth-mindfully" skill
    必须用这个术语代替"不增长"(因为后者会被误读为停滞)。
  tags: [core-concept, philosophy]

- id: g12
  term: Sell to Your First Hundred Customers
  type: term
  source_chapter: 第4章 Sell to Your First Hundred Customers
  author_definition: |
    "Selling is a discovery process, and minimalist entrepreneurs use sales as
    an opportunity to talk to potential customers one by one about their
    products while simultaneously educating themselves about the problem they
    are trying to solve for them. Selling this way is a long game built on
    relationships and vulnerability, not a one-day grand opening extravaganza
    followed by selling to strangers."
    "Manual 'sales' will be 99 percent of your growth in the early days, and
    word of mouth will be 99 percent of your growth in the latter days."
  key_distinction: |
    ≠ "找 100 个种子用户"或"早期采用者"
    ≠ "拉新/获客" funnel 上端的动作
    = 创始人亲自一对一完成 100 次销售——把每次销售当成
      用户研究和教育机会,而不是成交动作。
      "100" 是作者选定的里程碑数字。
  why_it_matters: |
    下游 "founder-led-sales" skill 必须明确:100 次里至少 80 次是创始人本人
    完成、且每次都是 discovery process。否则就退化成"cold email 模板"。
  tags: [core-concept, sequence, methodology]

- id: g13
  term: Product-Market Fit (redefined)
  type: term
  source_chapter: 第5章 Market by Being You
  author_definition: |
    "I define 'product-market fit' as having repeat customers who sign up and
    use your product on their own so that you can start to focus on outbound
    sales."
    重点在 "repeat" 和 "on their own"——客户不需要创始人继续外联,
    仍会持续回来买。
  key_distinction: |
    ≠ Andreessen 经典定义(好市场 + 好产品)
    ≠ "达到 PMF 之前不要做任何营销"
    = 一个可操作的判定:revenue is recurring without sales effort。
  why_it_matters: |
    这个可操作的定义让"何时从 sales 转到 marketing"有了明确边界。
    下游 skill 在判断"什么时候该做付费广告"时,必须以这个定义为门槛。
  tags: [core-concept, borrowed, reframe, milestone]

- id: g14
  term: Educate / Inspire / Entertain (三层内容漏斗)
  type: term
  source_chapter: 第5章 Market by Being You
  author_definition: |
    "I've learned that there are three levels to the type of content people
    share, each one with more potential reach than the last."
    Level 1 Educate:技术性教学,可触达"学生"。
    Level 2 Inspire:把经验上升到人生哲学,可触达"想活得更好的人"。
    Level 3 Entertain:用幽默和故事包装,触达"几乎所有人"。
    "When push comes to shove, entertainment wins."
  key_distinction: |
    ≠ "内容营销 = 写博客"
    ≠ "viral content = 标题党"
    = 内容从个人化向大众化的三级进阶,每一级受众规模指数级放大。
      作者明示:"Make your mistakes when few people are watching."
  why_it_matters: |
    下游 "authenticity-marketing" skill 必须给用户一个成长路径,
    不能一上来就要求"做出 entertaining 的内容"——那是 Level 3。
  tags: [core-concept, framework, content]

- id: g15
  term: Build in Public
  type: term
  source_chapter: 第2章 + 第5章
  author_definition: |
    Nathan Barry 挂在办公室的三条规则之一:"Work in Public / Teach Everything
    You Know / Create Every Day."作者引用并贯穿全书。
    第5章延伸:"Not only should you share what you learn to maintain your ties
    to your community, but you should also be building your business in public
    and sharing that process with your customers."
  key_distinction: |
    ≠ "open source"(公开代码)
    ≠ "influencer 营销"(单方面讲故事)
    = 在学习、犯错、做决策的全过程中,持续向社区和客户公开进度。
  why_it_matters: |
    这是连接 Community 阶段和 Marketing 阶段的桥梁动作。下游 skill 不能
    把 marketing 当成"等有产品再做"的事——Build in Public 从 community
    阶段就要开始。
  tags: [core-concept, methodology]

- id: g16
  term: Make Fans, Not Headlines
  type: term
  source_chapter: 第5章 Market by Being You
  author_definition: |
    "Marketing is not about making headlines, but making fans. Start by
    educating, then inspiring, then entertaining. Each of these three levels
    of content is more far-reaching than the last."
    "Instead of making headlines, they make fans—who turn themselves into
    customers over time."
  key_distinction: |
    ≠ PR / 媒体投放
    ≠ "viral growth"
    = 把每一个关注者变成长期的 fan,fan 自行转化为 customer,
      不是靠一次性曝光拉动购买。
  why_it_matters: |
    下游 marketing skill 的核心心智模型。如果误读为"如何上头条",
    整套方法论就退化成传统 PR。
  tags: [core-concept, philosophy]

- id: g17
  term: Profitable Confidence (借用并特定化)
  type: term
  source_chapter: 第6章 Grow Yourself and Your Business Mindfully
  author_definition: |
    借自 Wistia CEO Chris Savage。原文:"Chris Savage, CEO and cofounder of
    Wistia... calls the resulting sense of conviction 'profitable confidence.'
    In 2017, Savage and his cofounder, Brendan Schwartz, realized that their
    efforts to scale and grow quickly had not only made their work less
    creatively interesting, but had also made them unprofitable. By slowing
    down, they figured out how to trust their instincts again—and wound up
    more profitable than ever."
    作者延展:"For Wistia, being profitably confident means that Chris and
    Brendan know they will live no matter what they do... They don't have to
    bet the company anymore if they want to try something new."
  key_distinction: |
    ≠ "自信"(personal confidence,可与财务无关)
    ≠ "舒适区"(comfort zone)
    = 来自"盈利+可控成本"的安全感,允许做长周期实验而不赌公司命。
  why_it_matters: |
    这个术语把"盈利"翻译成心理/决策语言。下游 "mindful-growth" skill
    不能只讲财务,必须传达"盈利带来的勇气与从容"。
  tags: [core-concept, borrowed, psychology]

- id: g18
  term: Build the House You Want to Live In
  type: term
  source_chapter: 第7章 Build the House You Want to Live In
  author_definition: |
    "Before you're ready to hire anyone, you first need to make a company
    people want to work for. That begins with setting your values, preferably
    as early as possible."
    "Values are not generic two-word commandments that companies use to state
    the obvious. Quite the opposite: They're for stating the non-obvious, in
    non-obvious ways."
    核心论点:"Your job listings should be a filter, not a magnet. Most people
    won't enjoy working at your company, and your job listings should make it
    clear that they should look elsewhere."
  key_distinction: |
    ≠ "雇主品牌"(employer branding,通常是为了招更多人)
    ≠ "公司文化活动"(team building)
    = 从第一性原理定义工作方式,主动筛选和排斥不匹配的人。
      文化是差异化优势,不是成本中心。
  why_it_matters: |
    下游 "culture-design" skill 必须以"filter, not magnet"作为核心准则,
    并强调"价值观是双刃剑,会同时吸引和排斥人",这点与多数管理书相反。
  tags: [core-concept, philosophy, hiring]

- id: g19
  term: Default Alive / Default Dead (借用 Graham)
  type: term
  source_chapter: 第6章 Grow Yourself and Your Business Mindfully
  author_definition: |
    "Paul Graham, founder of Y Combinator, can size up a company immediately
    based on whether they're 'default alive or default dead.' If expenses
    and revenues stay constant, will the company live or die? Incredibly,
    half of the founders he talks to have no idea."
    作者把它作为 "Don't Spend Money You Don't Have" 的判定标准。
  key_distinction: |
    ≠ 简单的"现金流为正"
    = 一个静态判定:如果维持当前收支不变,公司会自然活下来还是死去?
  why_it_matters: |
    下游 "profitability-first" skill 必须给出一个"是否 default alive"的
    自检清单,而不仅仅是"利润为正"。
  tags: [borrowed, diagnostic, financial]

- id: g20
  term: ikigai (借用日本概念并本地化)
  type: term
  source_chapter: 第8章 Where Do We Go from Here?
  author_definition: |
    "This is the Japanese concept of ikigai, which aligns what you love,
    with what the world needs, with what you can be paid for, and with what
    you are good at."
    作者用它来说明"极简创业的终极目标不是做大,而是让自己的生命
    与事业对齐"。
  key_distinction: |
    ≠ 一般"职业规划"或"使命愿景"
